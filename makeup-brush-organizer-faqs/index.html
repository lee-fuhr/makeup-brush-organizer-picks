Makeup brush organizer questions answered

Updated June 2026Independently researchedNo paid placement.

Quick answer

The best makeup brush organizer depends on your brush collection size, vanity space, and whether you prioritize dust protection or easy access. Common materials like acrylic, ceramic, bamboo, and metal each have trade-offs in durability, weight, and cleaning. Knowing your brush lengths and how you store them (upright, drawer, or travel) will help you choose an organizer that keeps your tools clean and within reach.

What types of makeup brush organizers are there?

Countertop holders are the most popular, they sit on your vanity and keep brushes upright in slots or compartments. Materials range from clear acrylic (easy to see all brushes) to ceramic or metal (more decorative). Many have a weighted base to prevent tipping. Drawer organizers are designed to fit inside a makeup drawer, keeping brushes separate and protected from dust while freeing up counter space. They usually come as modular inserts that you can rearrange. Travel cases are roll-up pouches or zip-up kits with individual slots, ideal for protecting brush bristles in transit. Wall-mounted magnetic strips or pegboard organizers are a space-saving option that keeps brushes off the counter entirely.

Which material is best for a brush holder?

Acrylic is a top choice for visibility and easy cleaning, you can see every brush at a glance and wipe it down with gentle soap and water. However, it can scratch over time if not handled carefully. Ceramic and glass holders offer a heavier, more stylish option that resists tipping, but they are fragile and may not have individual brush compartments. Bamboo looks warm and natural but can absorb moisture if brushes are stored damp. Metal holders are durable and often come with a powder coating, but some may scratch brush handles. The right material depends on your decor, how often you move the holder, and your tolerance for maintenance.

How do I choose the right size brush organizer?

Start by measuring your longest face brush, powder brushes and kabuki brushes can be quite tall. A standard countertop organizer usually accommodates brushes up to 8 inches long, but some extra-deep models handle longer handles. For eye brushes and shorter tools, a compact tiered holder saves space. Consider the number of brushes you own. Many organizers have 5 to 10 slots, but larger ones can hold 20 or more. If you like to keep brushes separated by type (eye vs. face), look for divided compartments rather than a single open cup. Depth matters too: a shallow organizer might let shorter brushes slip under the rim, making them hard to grab. Check product photos or user reviews for real-life scale comparisons.

Can you clean a makeup brush organizer?

Yes, but the cleaning method depends on the material. Acrylic and glass can be washed with warm water and mild dish soap, then dried with a soft cloth to reduce scratches. Ceramic holders are generally dishwasher-safe if there are no metal accents, but hand washing is safer to preserve any paint or finish. Bamboo and unfinished wood should never be soaked, wipe them down with a damp cloth and dry immediately. Metal holders with a painted finish can be wiped with a gentle cleaner, but avoid abrasive sponges. For any organizer, remove the brushes first and let the holder air dry completely before returning your tools. Regular cleaning prevents dust, oils, and bacteria from building up inside the slots.

Do I need a covered or open organizer?

Covered organizers, those with a lid or dome, protect your brushes from dust, pet hair, and accidental splashes. They are especially useful if you keep your makeup out on an open vanity or share a bathroom. The downside is that you have to lift the cover every time you reach for a brush, which can be slightly less convenient. Open organizers give you instant access and often allow better airflow, helping brushes dry completely after cleaning. They also tend to be more compact and less expensive. If you’re diligent about dusting your vanity or store brushes in a drawer, an open design is perfectly fine. Some hybrid options have a partial cover or a rotating design that balances protection and accessibility.

What about travel brush organizers?

Travel brush organizers prioritize portability and brush protection. Roll-up cases made of nylon or faux leather have elastic loops to hold each brush securely, preventing bristle damage during transit. Zip-around cases with a hard shell offer more protection against crushing. For shorter trips, a compact upright pouch with a drawstring can keep brushes separate from other toiletries. Some travel organizers double as a countertop holder unrolled, look for a design that lays flat or stands up. The key is to match the organizer to your brush lengths: many travel options are designed for shorter eye brushes and a few face brushes, so check the loop size and case depth if you carry long-handled tools.

Frequently asked questions

How many brushes does a typical makeup brush organizer hold?

It varies widely. Small countertop holders often have 5 to 8 slots, while larger tiered or rotating models can hold 20 to 30 brushes. Drawer inserts usually accommodate a dozen or more, depending on the drawer size and brush length.

Can I put damp brushes in an organizer?

No, storing brushes while damp can trap moisture, leading to mold growth inside the holder and damage to brush ferrules (the metal band). Always let brushes dry completely, laid flat or bristle-side down, before returning them to any organizer.

Will an acrylic brush organizer scratch easily?

Acrylic can develop minor surface scratches from regular wiping and brush placement, especially if you use abrasive cloths or drag brush handles across the sides. To minimize scratches, use a soft microfiber cloth for cleaning and avoid sharp objects inside the slots. Many users still find acrylic acceptable for daily use.

How do I keep heavy or long brushes from falling over?

Look for an organizer with a weighted base or a low center of gravity, ceramic, glass, or dense acrylic models are more stable. Individual slots that grip each brush handle (rather than an open cup) also help prevent tipping. For very long brushes, choose a holder that is at least as tall as the brush handles.

Are there organizers for very long or oversized brush handles?

Yes, but you need to check the interior depth. Some countertop holders are designed to accommodate handles up to 9 or 10 inches, often labeled as “tall” or “deep.” User photos and measurements in reviews are the best way to confirm fit. Avoid shallow cups or narrow slots that might cramp oversized handles.

What's the best brush organizer for a small vanity or limited counter space?

Compact, tiered organizers or wall-mounted options work well. A tiered holder stacks brushes vertically so you can fit more without taking up extra footprint. Magnetic strips or pegboards attached to the wall keep brushes off the counter entirely. If you must use a countertop model, choose one with a small base that holds only your daily-use brushes.

In shortChoosing a makeup brush organizer comes down to your storage space, brush collection size, and how much dust protection you need. Acrylic holders are popular for visibility and easy maintenance, while ceramic and bamboo offer style and weight. Measure your longest brushes and count your daily-use tools before buying, that extra step will save you from an overcrowded or flimsy holder. No matter which you pick, regular cleaning of the organizer will keep your brushes in better shape for longer.
